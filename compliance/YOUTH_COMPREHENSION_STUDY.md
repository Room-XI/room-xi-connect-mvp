## Executive Summary

This study evaluates whether youth (ages 13-25) comprehend the 6-level mood metaphor system used in Room XI Connect's Mood Orb visualization. The system uses weather-inspired metaphors (Cold, Stormy, Foggy, Clear, Breezy, Aurora) instead of clinical terms to make mood tracking youth-friendly and non-judgmental.

**Success Criteria:** 80%+ comprehension rate across all age groups and demographics.

---

## 1. Background

### Why This Study?
Room XI Connect uses a 6-level mood system with metaphorical labels to:
- Avoid clinical/diagnostic language that may stigmatize youth
- Make mood tracking accessible to all literacy levels
- Provide culturally-sensitive, non-judgmental framing

### Research Question
Can youth aged 13-25 accurately identify and explain the 6 mood levels using only the visual orb and legend?

---

## 2. Study Design

### Participants
- **Sample Size:** 30+ youth
- **Age Range:** 13-25 (10 per age group: 13-15, 16-18, 19-25)
- **Recruitment:** Partner organizations (CanManDan, JumpStart, YMCA Northern Alberta)
- **Compensation:** $15 gift card per participant
- **Consent:** Parental consent for under-16, youth assent for all

### Inclusion Criteria
- Age 13-25
- English or French speaking
- Access to smartphone or computer
- No prior use of Room XI Connect

### Exclusion Criteria
- Current participation in Room XI Connect pilot programs (to avoid bias)

---

## 3. Methodology

### Phase 1: Visual Recognition Test (5 minutes)
**Task:** Show participants the Mood Orb with 6 mood states. Ask them to name each mood from visual appearance alone.

**Materials:**
- 6 orb screenshots (one per mood state)
- No labels, only visual gradients and colors

**Scoring:**
- 1 point per correct mood identification
- Partial credit if description matches mood intent (e.g., "sad" for Cold)

---

### Phase 2: Verbal Explanation (10 minutes)
**Task:** For each mood, ask participants to:
1. Describe what that mood feels like
2. Give an example of when they might feel that way
3. Rate how clear the metaphor is (1-5 scale)

**Interview Questions:**
1. "What does Cold weather feel like to you emotionally?"
2. "Can you think of a time you felt this way?"
3. "Does this label make sense for this feeling?" (1=confusing, 5=very clear)

---

### Phase 3: Preference & Suggestions (5 minutes)
**Task:** Ask participants:
1. "Which mood label is most clear to you?"
2. "Which mood label is most confusing?"
3. "Would you change any of these labels? If so, to what?"
4. "Do these labels feel respectful and non-judgmental?"

---

## 4. Data Collection

### Quantitative Data
- Visual recognition accuracy (0-6 score)
- Clarity ratings per mood (1-5 scale)
- Overall comprehension rate (%)

### Qualitative Data
- Verbal explanations (transcribed)
- Alternative label suggestions
- Feedback on respectfulness and cultural sensitivity

---

## 5. Expected Results

### Hypotheses
1. **H1:** Youth will achieve 80%+ visual recognition accuracy
2. **H2:** "Clear" and "Breezy" will rate highest for clarity
3. **H3:** "Aurora" and "Cold" may require additional explanation
4. **H4:** No significant comprehension difference across age groups
5. **H5:** Youth will rate labels as respectful and non-judgmental (avg 4+/5)

---

## 6. Analysis Plan

### Quantitative Analysis
- Calculate mean comprehension rate (% correct identifications)
- Compare across age groups (13-15, 16-18, 19-25)
- Calculate mean clarity rating per mood
- Identify moods with <4.0/5 clarity rating

### Qualitative Analysis
- Code verbal explanations for themes
- Categorize alternative label suggestions
- Analyze cultural sensitivity feedback

---

## 7. Contingency Plans

### If Comprehension Rate < 80%:
1. **Option A:** Update mood labels based on participant suggestions
2. **Option B:** Add brief explanatory text to legend
3. **Option C:** Include onboarding tutorial explaining each mood

### If Specific Moods Confusing:
- **Cold:** Consider alternatives (Blue, Down, Low)
- **Aurora:** Consider alternatives (Peak, Brilliant, Radiant)
- **Foggy:** Consider alternatives (Hazy, Unsure, Confused)

- Small sample size (30 participants)
- Limited to English-speaking youth in Alberta
