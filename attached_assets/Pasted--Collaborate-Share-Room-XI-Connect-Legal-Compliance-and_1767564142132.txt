2. Legal Framework for Youth-Facing Apps
Your application's operations fall under several key pieces of legislation. Adherence is not optional and is fundamental to building trust with parents, youth, and partner organizations.
PIPEDA and Alberta's PIPA
These acts govern how private-sector organizations in Canada collect, use, and disclose personal information. For Room XI Connect, the most crucial principle is meaningful consent. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) specifies that for consent to be meaningful, organizations must emphasize four key elements upfront:
What personal information is being collected.
Who it is being shared with (including third parties).
For what purposes it is being collected, used, or disclosed.
The risk of harm and other consequences [2].
Furthermore, for youth, the OPC states that for children under 13, consent must be obtained from a parent or guardian. For teens (13-18), their own consent can be valid, but the process must be adapted to their level of maturity [2].
Alberta's Health Information Act (HIA)
As your app collects data related to mental health and wellness, it may be subject to the HIA, which imposes stricter rules for handling health information. This requires explicit, separate consent for the collection of any health-related data and mandates robust safeguards to protect that information [3].

## 7) `server/routes/auth.js` (big one)

Adds the missing endpoints your frontend was calling:

### `POST /api/auth/reset-password`

* Accepts email
* If user exists:

  * generates random token
  * stores **sha256 hash** of token in `password_reset_tokens`
  * expiry 1 hour
  * emails reset link: `/auth/update-password?token=...`
* Always returns `{ success: true }` even if email doesn’t exist (prevents email enumeration)

### `POST /api/auth/update-password`

* Accepts `{ token, newPassword }`
* Hashes token and finds valid unused record
* Updates user password hash (bcrypt)
* Marks token as used

**Why:** completes password reset end-to-end safely.

⚠️ Note: the patch as shown had an **import collision** (`eq` imported twice / mixed imports). That would need a small cleanup to compile cleanly.
